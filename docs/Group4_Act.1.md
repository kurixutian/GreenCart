II. Overview of the Selected Case Study
A. Background of the Organization

The Philippine Health Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth) is a government-owned and controlled
corporation established under Republic Act No. 7875, also known as the National Health Insurance Act of
1995.  PhilHealth  is  responsible  for  administering  the National Health Insurance Program (NHIP), which
aims to provide Filipinos with access to affordable and quality healthcare services.

As  the  country's  national  health  insurance  provider, PhilHealth processes millions of healthcare
claims from hospitals, clinics, and other accredited healthcare institutions. Over the years, the increasing
volume  of  claims  created  challenges  in  processing,  storing,  and  managing  large  amounts  of  data  and
documents. To address these issues, PhilHealth introduced the Electronic Claims System (eClaims) using
a  Hybrid  Approach  in  2017.  This initiative combined electronic submission and processing with existing
systems  to  improve  efficiency  while  allowing  healthcare  providers  to  gradually  transition  to  digital
operations.

B. Purpose of the Consolidation Initiative

The  primary  purpose  of  implementing  the  Electronic  Claims  System  (eClaims)  using  a  Hybrid
Approach  was  to  streamline  and  modernize  PhilHealth's  claims  processing  system.  Prior  to  the
implementation, many claims were processed manually, resulting in delays, duplicate records, increased
paperwork, and higher administrative costs.

Through the consolidation of claims data and the integration of electronic processes, PhilHealth sought to:

●  Reduce the processing time of healthcare claims.
●  Minimize errors caused by manual encoding and document handling.
●
Improve the accuracy and accessibility of claims information.
●  Decrease reliance on physical paper documents and storage.
●  Enhance coordination between PhilHealth and accredited healthcare providers.
●  Support the government's digital transformation initiatives in public service delivery.

The hybrid approach allowed healthcare institutions with varying levels of technological capability
to participate in the system while gradually adapting to fully electronic transactions. As a result, PhilHealth
improved operational efficiency, strengthened data management practices, and provided faster and more
reliable services to its members and healthcare partners.

IV. Analysis of Reduction Outcomes
A. Reduction in Physical Paper Archives
Discussion:

The circular aims to minimize warehousing of claims forms and supporting documents. Only the
Claim  Signature  Form  requires  physical  signing  and  scanning,  while  other  documents  are  digitally
scanned and retained at HCI or service-provider facilities, reducing paper storage at PhilHealth offices.

B. Reduction in Redundant Data Storage Hardware
Discussion:

Retiring PHICS/SCLAIMS and physical storage media eliminated redundant facility-level storage.
Claims  data  now  flows  through  the  eCEWS/PSDG  gateway  into  PhilHealth’s  central  data  center  or
accredited  service  providers.  This  consolidates  previously  dispersed  records  into  a  smaller  number  of
standards-compliant repositories.

C. Qualitative Benefits
Explanation:

The  circular  also  aims  to  improve  fraud  detection,  prevent  duplicate  encoding,  streamline
workflows,  and  automate  claim  validation.  This  allows  staff  to  focus  on  auditing,  speeds up payments,
reduces returned claims, and improves service quality and eligibility verification for members.

V. Implementation Challenges
A. Technical Challenges
Analysis:

One major technical challenge is the need for healthcare institutions to use software that meets
PhilHealth's  technical  requirements  and  certiﬁcation  standards.  Institutions  with  existing  Hospital
Information  Systems  (HIS)  or  Electronic  Medical  Records  (EMR)  may  need  to  modify or integrate their
systems  for  electronic  transmission.  The  system  also  requires  secure  data  transmission,  proper  data
formats,  encryption,  and  reliable  connectivity.  These  requirements  can  make  implementation  more
diﬃcult, especially for institutions with limited technical resources. PhilHealth are primarily connected to
the modernization of its digital healthcare infrastructure. Rather than being isolated computer problems,
these  issues  inﬂuence  claim  processing,  data  security,  communication  with  healthcare  providers,  and
public access to health insurance services. PhilHealth's continuing digital transformation aims to address
these  concerns  by replacing legacy systems, strengthening cybersecurity, and improving interoperability
across  the  national  healthcare  system.  The  success  of  these  technical  improvements  will  play  an
important role in delivering faster, safer, and more reliable health insurance services to Filipinos

C. Organizational and Cultural Challenges
Analysis:

PhilHealth’s  Electronic  Claims  System required healthcare institutions to shift from paper-based
claims processing to electronic processing. This change required employees to adjust to new workflows,
learn  the  system  through training, and use certified eClaims software. Some employees and healthcare
providers may also need time to adapt to the new digital process.

During  the  implementation,  healthcare  providers  raised  concerns  about  system  readiness,
implementation  deadlines,  and  agreements  with  technology  providers.  Some  requested  more  time  to
comply  and  features  such  as  offline  functionality.  These  challenges  show  that  successful  digital
transformation  needs  not  only  technology  but  also  proper  training,  employee  adaptation,  and
organizational readiness.

VI. Evaluation of Effectiveness
Cost Savings

PhilHealth’s  Electronic  Claims  System  helped  reduce  costs  by  minimizing  manual  encoding,
duplicate data entry, and the handling of physical documents. It also reduced the time and effort needed
to  process  claims.  However,  healthcare  institutions  still  needed  to  spend  on  software,  equipment,  and
employee training during the implementation.

Space Savings

The  system  helped  reduce  the  need  for  physical  storage  because  claims  and  supporting
documents  could  be  submitted  and  processed  electronically.  This  minimized  the  amount  of  paper  that
healthcare institutions needed to keep and store, allowing them to use less physical space for filing and
archiving documents.

Data Accessibility

The  Electronic  Claims  System made claims information easier and faster to access. Healthcare
institutions  could  electronically  submit  claims,  check  patient  eligibility,  and  track  claim  status  without
relying heavily on physical documents. This improved record retrieval and helped make claims processing
more efficient.

Data Security

PhilHeath  uses  a  centralized  database  system  that  improves  the  security  of  the  member's
records.  By  storing  it  in  a  controlled  environment,  information  are  stored  digitally  instead  of  physical
documents.
It  helps  protect  the  sensitive  information  of  members  with  authentication  and  security
encryption  and  also  regular  monitoring  of  the  system.  It  prevents  unauthorized  access  or  even loss of
data. Compared to physical documents which are not able to be backed up or restored when damaged,
the digitally stored information can easily be backed up and recovered.

Operational Efficiency

The  implementation  of  eClaims significantly improves operational efficiency by reducing manual
data  entry,  duplication  of  work,  and  physical  submission  of  claim  documents.  It  allows  HCIs  to
electronically  submit  claims,  verify  eligibility,  track claim status, and receive reimbursements. PhilHealth
also  identified  improved  turnaround  time,  automated  workflows,  better  data  quality,  and  reduced
document storage as major benefits of the system.

Long-Term Sustainability

Interim  submission  portals  protect  smaller  facilities  from  immediate  system  upgrade  costs. Full
data  harmonization  bridges  communication  between  PhilHealth  and  national  health  systems.  Lower
physical  storage  demands  reduce  ongoing  facility  maintenance  expenses  significantly.  Scalable  digital
infrastructure  accommodates  future  public  health  expansions  across  the  country.  This  framework
establishes a self-sustaining foundation for modern healthcare governance.

VII. Conclusion and Recommendations
Conclusion

The  implementation  of  the  PhilHealth  Electronic  Claims  System  (eClaims)  using  a  hybrid
approach represents an important step toward improving the efficiency and reliability of healthcare claims
processing  in  the  Philippines.  The  system  reduces  manual  encoding  and  paperwork,  improves  the
accuracy and validation of claims information, and facilitates faster electronic submission and processing
of claims. It also supports better coordination between PhilHealth and health care institutions.

However,  the  effectiveness  of  eClaims  depends  on  the  availability  of  appropriate  technology,
reliable systems, trained personnel, and compliance with data security and privacy requirements. Overall,
the  implementation  of  eClaims  has  the  potential  to  make  the  claims  process  more  efficient,  accurate,
secure, and sustainable while supporting PhilHealth's transition toward a more digital healthcare system.

Recommendations
Based on the analysis, the following recommendations are proposed:

●  Strengthen  the  eClaims  system  –  PhilHealth  should  continuously  improve  the  system  to  make

claims submission, validation, and processing faster and more reliable.

●  Provide  continuous  training  and  technical  support  –  Health  care  institutions  should  receive
adequate  training  and  assistance  to  ensure  that  employees  can  properly use and maintain the
eClaims system.
Improve data security and privacy – Strong security measures should be implemented to protect
members' personal and medical information from unauthorized access, loss, or misuse.

●

●  Ensure  system  interoperability  –  PhilHealth  should  continue  improving  compatibility  between
eClaims and the information systems of different health care institutions to promote smooth and
accurate data exchange.

●  Conduct  regular  evaluation  –  PhilHealth  should  regularly  assess  the  system's  data  security,
operational  efficiency,  and  long-term  sustainability  to  identify  problems  and  opportunities  for
improvement.

●  Support  institutions  with  limited  technological  resources  –  Additional  assistance  should  be
provided to health care institutions that lack sufficient hardware, software, internet connectivity, or
technical personnel to implement eClaims effectively.
